# Convention profile: the lens

**What it is.** A lens is a saved way of reading a world. The 22 types say what a world holds. A profile says what a convention IS (a message, a room element, a work item: its fields and what every reader must agree on). A lens says what to READ (which things, how far, how much). Every tool already has an unsaved one (the context skill's recipe, a pack's choice, Atlas's filters, what an NPC may talk about); a lens makes it one element every tool reads. A convention on the 22 types: nothing here adds a type or a field to the standard, and a tool that has never read this page sees an ordinary Construct with one extension key it keeps byte for byte.

**What is core is not a lens; it is the seat.** Every reading starts from the same four things, whatever the world: who I am (my Character's sheet), what names me, my mail, what changed since I last read. That is the draught's base today and nobody configures it. Lenses add to the seat and never take from it. **A lens only narrows**: what a seat starts with is the lens applied inside what the seat may already read, never past it. So a lens is never a way around a guest slice, and the security story is one sentence.

**What it is not.** A profile (what a thing is); a policy in the old sense (what a seat may: that is the rung and the seat); the seat's core; a graph (a graph draws a set; a lens produces one). Nor a graph profile (nodes, edges, how a Relation is drawn), a map-space profile (what a pixel means), or a world's "now" (`as_of` here points at it).

**The pair.** Profile `lens`, the pair `construct` + supertype `Lens`, the type and the supertype each trimmed and folded like the room profile's pairs (`profiles/room.md`): `" lens "` matches, `"Lenses"` does not. A lens is a **Construct**: its definition fits ("abstract or conceptual structures that help explain or organize how the world works") and its fields are the lens's fields already: `founder` (who made it), `custodian` (the Institution that keeps it), `status` (draft, live, retired), `reach` (who it is for), `rationale` (why it exists, in words), and under Involves a link list to every type that can be an anchor. **Supertype `Lens`, subtype free** (a pack may set it: `Mail`, `Role`, `Chapter`); the subtype `Template` is reserved (*The template*, below).

**The anchors.** **The links are the anchors and nothing else**: "Product" links the Product institution, the roadmap construct and the two people who own it. The anchor fields are the Construct's Involves links in the schema's order, less `titles`: `characters`, `objects`, `locations`, `species`, `creatures`, `institutions`, `traits`, `collectives`, `zones`, `abilities`, `phenomena`, `languages`, `families`, `relations`, `constructs`, `events`, `narratives`. `founder` and `custodian` say who made and keeps the lens and are never anchors.
- **The umbrella.** A lens that links other lenses through `constructs` is the umbrella ("Office" = Mail + People + Decisions). A Construct in `constructs` that is not a lens is an anchor like any other.
- **A Title's defaults.** `titles` on a lens names the Titles it is the default for ("everyone with the Engineer title starts with Product and Decisions"). They are not anchors.

**Where a seat's choice lives.** A seat's own choice of lenses is the seat's, not the world's: it lives on the membership in keel, where the dashboard edits it and the draught reads it, like a seat's Character today; no Relation per use. A folder world has no membership, so the Character carries `x_lens_uses` (a list of lens ids, a key of this profile) for an AI reading the folder (the context skill); Atlas lists every lens in the world and keeps the view choice on the computer. When both exist, the membership wins: keel reads `x_lens_uses` only when the membership has no choice, and the dashboard saves a choice to the membership, never to the key, so the list is never kept in two places. A seat's choice has two states, never confused: a seat whose lenses were never set (null) starts with the lenses its Character's Titles name (a Title's defaults, below); a seat that chose an empty list has chosen nothing and gets no lens from its Titles; keel's roster says which as `lenses_from` (`chosen`, `titles`).

**Three levels and no more**: the world's shared lenses (made by the owner or a keeper, custodian the company), a Title's defaults (through `titles` on the lens), a seat's own choice (which shared lenses it uses, plus one private lens of its own). Public or not is the element's own visibility, nothing extra. **The pin is version zero**: a pinned row is a lens with the pinned elements as anchors, `depth` 0, the seat's own private lens.

**The template.** **A template is a lens with no anchors and subtype `Template`**: types, depth, window, show, mail, and nothing pointed at; it resolves to nothing until copied and pointed. Copied into a world and pointed at that world's things, it is a lens. That is how a lens travels between worlds and people: it is an element, it exports with a world, and a template library is a world whose elements are templates. A reader resolves a lens whose subtype is `Template` to nothing, anchors or not; pointing a copy means giving it anchors and another subtype.

**The field.** The recipe that is not links lives in one extension field, `x_lens`, a JSON object on the lens. Every key is optional; `null` reads as absent; an empty `x_lens` (or none) is a lens of depth 1 over its anchors with no window and the draught's default budget. An empty string is present, never absent: a key whose value is `""` where the table expects a word (`undated`, `show`, `mail`, a `types` entry) makes the lens unreadable, the key named; the element fields' convention that an empty value is unset does not reach into `x_lens`. A key this page does not name is ignored (a later serial may add one). A value outside the table makes the lens unreadable: the reader names the key and resolves nothing, never a guess. A whole-number key (`depth`, `budget`, `window`, `from`, `to`, `changed_within`) refuses a fraction (`7.5`) everywhere, refuses an integral float (`7.0`) where a reader can see the number's text, and reads it whole where it cannot (a platform limit, not a rule); a writer never writes an integral float. The prefix `x_lens_` is reserved; the one key under it today is `x_lens_leads_to`, a list of lens ids, the sign's "leads to", declared a link for the graph (below) and no part of the recipe. The keys are the draught's own words where it has one, with the draught's meaning:

| Key | What | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `depth` | how far from the anchors, in hops | 0 (the anchors only), 1 (and what links to or from them; the default), 2 (the profile allows it; a reader serves 0 and 1 and counts what the second hop reaches as `hops_not_served`) |
| `types` | which types may be kept | a list of the API's lowercase type slugs (`character`); a room pair as `narrative/message` beside `narrative` (the room profile's nine pairs, type and supertype lowercase, a slash between), and a reading aid's pair the same way (`law/charter`, `construct/guide`, `construct/domain`; rule 4: the pair and the mark `x_charter_of`; it binds what a hop reaches, and an anchor that is a reading aid is kept and followed whatever `types` says); each word trimmed and folded; empty or absent = any type, and no room element or reading aid |
| `window` | how far back | a whole number from 0 of the world's own time units before the reading's `as_of` (a world that counts years counts its window in years): an element is inside when its date is at or after `as_of` minus the window. Absent = always. Never beside `from` or `to` |
| `from` | an absolute range's start, in the world's dates | a whole number, inclusive; alone, the range runs to `as_of` |
| `to` | the range's end | a whole number after `from`, exclusive (the range is closed-open); never alone; a `to` after `as_of` is clipped to `as_of`: the fence stays the upper edge |
| `undated` | what the fence does with an element of a dated type that carries no date | `include` (the default: kept) or `exclude` (cut and counted `undated`) |
| `changed_within` | how recently changed | a whole number of days from 1; an element whose `updated_at` is older than that many days before the reading's wall clock is cut and counted `stale`; absent means no cut. It reads the server's `updated_at` (a folder element's `server_updated_at` or `local_updated_at`, the newer); it is a third clock beside `window` (the world's own time) and `undated` (world dates), and composes with both |
| `show` | how much of each kept element | `full` (the body as the reader is served it; the default), `first_line` (`id`, `type`, `name`, and `description` up to its first line break), `name` (`id`, `type`, `name`) |
| `budget` | the limit | a whole number from 1 to 1000, counted in elements; default 50, the draught's |
| `mail` | the mail rule | `unread_full_read_titles` (unread in full, read as subject, sender, date; a rolling window; one totals line), `all` (every message in full, the same window and totals), or `none` (the default) |
| `notes` | a sentence per anchor | an object from an anchor's id to one sentence, the lens's reason for that anchor; a kept anchor's row carries it as `note`; a note for an id that is not an anchor is ignored and counted (only one the reader can see: a note keyed to an element it cannot see is no note of its); a note for an anchor the reader cannot see is dropped with the anchor and named in `x_served_trimmed`. Not a second description: the lens's `description` says what the lens is for, a note says why this anchor is in it |

**Resolution: the rule for readers.** The reader is one pure function over a world snapshot, `resolveLens(world, lens, reader)`, which returns the kept elements in order, the cut counted by reason, and, when the lens names a mail rule, the mail part. It never writes.
1. **Visibility before resolution.** The resolver takes the reader's own view of the world; a hidden or proposed element is not there to hop through. Keel's draught already works this way: every gather is scoped to the reader, and an item counts only if the body that reader is served carries the link. A known-of stub is in the view: it is kept as served and nothing hops out of it (a stub carries no links). Nor is it read for what it does not serve: an umbrella's child the reader holds only as a stub is not entered (it is an anchor the reader cannot see, rule 6), and a Message the reader knows only as a stub, which serves no `characters`, is never read as addressed to everyone (rule 9 reads whole messages only). A lens the reader is served only as a known-of stub (`known_of: true`: a name and first-line facts, no links, no `x_lens`) resolves as not there: there is nothing to read, and a stub is never a filter over the reader's view. A proposed element is in no reader's view: one whose `x_review_proposed` holds any value but null (absent or null is no proposal), the review profile's rule for a reader that hands a world on (`profiles/review.md`: a doubt is left out with a mark), since a resolver hands a world to an AI.
2. **A hop reaches both ways**, to and from an anchor: one hop from an element reaches every element its served body links to and every element whose served body links to it.
3. **A cycle, and the umbrella**: only the umbrella's own `constructs` links are entered, each child once (a visited set), the union in link order, each child resolved by its own recipe; a Lens reached by a hop is kept as an element and never entered; a lens that only reaches itself resolves to its anchors. The umbrella's own anchors come first, then each child's kept list, each element once; the umbrella's `budget` caps the union. The lens being read, and every lens entered, is never in its own result. Each child keeps at most its own `budget`, the rest counted `budget`, before the union takes the umbrella's mail rule and budget; `show` is the umbrella's alone, the rows made once from the union. A child's `notes` never reach a row: the rows and their notes are the umbrella's. A child's own mail rule takes its messages out of the child's kept, and they are neither the umbrella's mail part nor counted under it. "Its own result" means that lens's own: a lens is never in the result it resolves, and a sibling lens a child's hop reaches is an ordinary kept element of the result it was reached into, even when the umbrella enters it as another child. The union drops an element only by its id, never because it was entered, so a reader that drops entered lens ids from the union reads it wrong; a sibling entered before the hop is never reached.
4. **A hop never crosses a room element** into lore or out of it; a lens that wants the room names a room pair in `types` (`narrative/message`); an anchor that is itself a room element is kept, and nothing hops out of it. A room element a hop reaches is kept only when `types` names its pair, and is cut as `types` otherwise. The reading aids stand beside the room pairs (the room profile's charter, guide and domains: `law/charter`, `construct/guide`, `construct/domain`, each matched by the pair, trimmed and folded, and the mark `x_charter_of`, the id of the world the aid belongs to; the pair without the mark is lore, a kingdom's Charter in a novel's world among them): an anchor that is a marked reading aid is kept and its links are followed like any anchor's (unlike a room anchor: a lens pointed at a Domain means its hubs); the pair-and-mark rule binds only an aid a hop reaches, which is kept only when `types` names its pair and is cut as `types` otherwise, and a hop never continues out of such a reached aid unless the pair is named.
5. **The budget's order**: anchors first in the lens's own link order (the schema's field order, then each field's array order), then each hop's elements by type in the schema's order, then within a type newest first (`updated_at`), then by id (by id alone, a limit can keep the old version of a fact and cut the new); the cut is reported as a count by reason, never silent. The schema's type order is `schema.json`'s: ability, collective, character, construct, creature, event, family, institution, language, law, location, map, marker, narrative, object, phenomenon, pin, relation, species, title, trait, zone. The budget keeps the first elements in this order and cuts the rest.
6. **An anchor the reader cannot see** is skipped, never an error. The count ("1 anchor not visible") is the owner's view only; every other reader gets the lens as if the anchor were not there, since the count would tell a guest that something hidden exists. A lens that names an anchor is never read as a filter, whoever reads it: one whose only anchors are hidden from the reader resolves to nothing. When keel serves a lens to a reader that cannot see an anchor, it leaves that id out and names the field in `x_served_trimmed`, as it does for a message's recipients (keel marks messages today; a lens's mark ships with the draught's reading of lenses); a reader holding only served bodies reads a lens whose `x_served_trimmed` names an anchor field as anchored. The anchor's note goes with it: keel drops that anchor's entry from `x_lens`'s `notes` and names `notes` in `x_served_trimmed` beside the anchor field, and drops a note keyed to any other element the reader cannot see; a reader holding served bodies never re-hides, as for a message's recipients.
7. **A lens with no anchors and only types** is a filter over the whole visible world, unless it is a template: a template carries subtype `Template` and resolves to nothing; a live lens with no anchors and no `Template` subtype is a filter. A filter keeps, in the order of rule 5, every element of its `types` the reader can see; elements of other types are not selected and not counted.
8. **The upper edge is keel's `as_of` fence**: the doors sit on `as_of`, the reading's date (absent: the world's now), not on `window`, which only reaches back. A reading with no `as_of` in a world with no now has no fence, and its `window` has nothing to measure from, so it cuts nothing; `from` and `to` still apply. A known-of stub serves no date, so the resolver fences on the element's own date, never on the stub's null (an Event after `as_of` reached as a stub is cut, `after_as_of`). `undated` is a key of `x_lens`: under the fence an undated element is kept (`include`, the draught's default, as the fence's doors serve undated Events and undated knowledge Narratives) or cut and counted ("undated: 3") when the lens says `exclude`, so the leak becomes a counted cut. The **date table**, type to its date field: `birth_date` (Character, Creature), `founding_date` (Institution, Location), `formation_date` (Collective), `date` (Law), `grant_date` (Title), `start_date` (Construct, Event, Narrative, Relation, Zone); the ten types with no date field (Ability, Family, Language, Map, Marker, Object, Phenomenon, Pin, Species, Trait) are timeless and never fenced. The fence, `undated`, `window`, `from` and `to` all read this table. The fence and `undated` apply to anchors too; `types`, `window` and `changed_within` apply to what a hop or a filter reaches, never to an anchor the lens names. An element the fence cuts `after_as_of`, an anchor among them, is not hopped out of: nothing is reached through what lies past the reading's date. A date compares at its stated precision and is never filled to a day; every date field in the standard is a whole number in the world's `time_basic_unit`, so its precision is the world's unit. A Character's own sheet is never fenced, and that is not a lens case: the seat is core and no lens takes from it.
9. **The mail rule reads the message profile** (`profiles/message.md`): unread by the reader's own Read mark, the sender verified; the totals line counts only what this reader may see. The mail part holds the messages addressed to the reader's Character and not sent by it, in `created_at` order; the window is thirty days on `created_at` (mail is dated by the clock, not the world's calendar) before the reading's time, capped by the lens's `budget` (the newest kept, the oldest cut first and counted, each by reason); unread messages in full, read ones as a title (`id`, `name`, `narrator`, `created_at`), or every one in full under `all`. The totals count the unread and the read the reader may see, before the window and the cap, so the cut says how many older messages the world's messages route still holds. A message in the mail part is not repeated among the kept. A reader with no Character has no mail.
10. **Totals and counts are the reader's**, never the world's: "212 more" is 212 the reader could open.

**A Relation is traversed as an edge**: a hop that lands on a Relation also reaches every element the Relation's own link fields name, in the same hop (never elements that link to the Relation, never on through a second Relation in that hop); a Relation that is itself an anchor reaches its parties at hop 1, as any element does. The Relation carries the hop whether or not `types` keeps it; a party the reader cannot see is not reached.

**The cut reasons, one closed list.** Each cut element is counted once, under the first reason that applies (the fence, then `types`, then the window, then `changed_within`, then the budget):
- `budget`: past the limit, in the order of rule 5.
- `types`: reached, of a type (or a room pair, or a reading aid's pair) the lens does not keep.
- `window`: dated before the window, or outside `from` and `to`.
- `stale`: changed longer ago than `changed_within` days before the reading's wall clock.
- `undated`: of a dated type with no date, under `exclude`.
- `after_as_of`: dated after the reading's `as_of`, by the element's own date.
- `anchor_not_visible`: an anchor the reader cannot see. The owner's count only.
- `not_visible`: a non-anchor a hop would reach that the reader's view leaves out (hidden by its keeper, outside a guest's slice, or proposed). The owner's count only: the owner reading as another reader, or as itself, where it counts what is proposed.
- `hops_not_served`: what a hop past depth 1 would reach, less a room element or a reading aid whose pair `types` does not name (a hop never counts what rule 4 would stop).

A reader that is not the owner never carries `anchor_not_visible` or `not_visible`, not even as zero.

**The invariants, each with its test.**
- **A lens's links are filtered per reader like any other link**; a public lens never shows a private anchor's name. Test: a planted lens built to leak, drawn as owner, contributor, guest and a seat; each result a subset of the owner's; the planted name red before the walk is believed (the fixture's planted world; keel's hidden-mark walk, the lens version).
- **A public picture of a lens is drawn from the public read only**: the cut-and-why list is owner-only; the public view shows only what the public reader's own resolution keeps; text leaks what a link cannot, so counts and reasons are the reader's. Test: bake the public picture and page twice, with the private anchor in the world and without; the two are byte-identical; a guest's or keyless answer never holds "not visible" or a reason count for one (the fixture holds the reading's half; the bake is the picture's own test).
- **The hop stops at the reader's edge, not at the count.** Test: public A, hidden P, public B; anchor A, two hops; the guest never gets B (its only path crosses P), the owner does; P's absence never moves the guest's counts (the planted world: the owner counts B as `hops_not_served` until a second hop is served).
- **A lens drawn does not move the world**: the layout is baked per world, a lens changes weight and visibility. Test: the baked layout's bytes before and after any lens are equal (the fixture holds the reading's half: the world is identical before and after every reading).
- **One truth**: a seat's lenses are read from keel by every tool; the dashboard has no second editor. Test: the dashboard's sign-in walk asserts the page writes to no route but the named ones, and the lenses it shows equal keel's resolved route for the same seat (the fixture holds where the choice lives: the membership wins).

**The graph: a lens's reading as nodes and edges.** A graph is a reading of a world as nodes and edges: the elements a reader sees are the nodes, the links between them are the edges. It adds nothing to the world: no node or edge is ever stored, and a drawing never changes or adds a link. What may travel with a world is a layout's place, as a Map with Pins, which is the map space profile's, not this one's. The graph lives on this page because a graph is what a lens shows: with a lens, the kept become the nodes, and the reader, the cut and the fixture worlds are the same.
- **The reader.** `worldGraph(world, reader, options)`, one pure function over the same snapshot `resolveLens` reads, with the same `reader` (the rung, the Character, a guest's `whole` and `known_of`, `as`); the Python package's is `world_graph`, the same in snake case. With `options.lens` (a lens id, or a lens body) the nodes are exactly the lens's `kept`, in its order before the graph's own sort, and the lens's `cut` rides along unchanged; the lens read is never a node (resolution rule 3: a lens is never in its own result), and the mail part is not kept (rule 9), so its messages are not nodes either; a lens that wants messages as nodes names the room pair in `types`, where they become kept. Without a lens the nodes are every element the reader sees, less what is out by default (below), and `cut` counts those out-by-default elements the reader sees, by their reason. A lens named by an id the reader cannot see answers no nodes and no edges with `unreadable` null, as a lens that keeps nothing does. A lens the reader cannot read answers `unreadable` with the key that broke, `resolveLens`'s own, and no nodes or edges; an unlensed graph's `unreadable` is null.
- **Nodes.** One per element, as `{id, type, name, family, icon}`, with `family` and `icon` read from `presentation.json` (a tool never looks them up; colour stays the tool's). A node that a flag brought back says so in `marks`: `room`, `hidden`, `proposed`; every mark that applies is carried, lens-kept or not. A guest's known-of element is a node with `known_of: true` and no edges out, since a stub carries no links. Positions are not part of the graph: a tool that bakes a layout keys it by id, never by name.
- **Edges.** One per element, field and target, as `{source, field, target, index}`, where `index` is the target's place in the field's list as the reader is served it (0 for a single link): the owner as stored, every other reader less the ids outside their view, so a hidden target is never a gap. **Which fields make an edge**: the schema's link fields, plus an extension field only where a published profile declares it a link, a closed table on this page: `x_lens_leads_to` (this profile: a list of lens ids, the sign's "leads to", the one link this profile adds) and `x_spine_depends_on` (the spine profile); both are indexed as stored for every reader, since the lens reader serves them untrimmed. **Both ends as stored**: a link held on both ends (`relation.events`, `event.relations`) is two edges, never merged, and a one-way link stays one-way; a drawing may draw a pair as one line, the reading never does. **Only between nodes**: an edge whose target is not a node is left out and counted in `edges_cut` by the reason its target is not a node (below); a link to an id that is not in the world is dropped and not counted, a dangling link, not a visibility fact. **A Relation is a node** with one edge per party, from its own link fields (the n-ary pattern); a drawing may fold one that links exactly two elements into a single line carrying its name and size it by `intensity`, and the data never folds. **No weight beyond `intensity` in the reading**: the data carries only what the schema holds; size and colour are the drawing's, never the reading's.
- **Edges cut, one closed list**, each counted once by the reason its target is not a node: the graph's out-by-default reasons (`room`, `hidden`, `proposed`, `map_space`, `reading_aid`); a lens's own cut reasons, for a target the lens cut; `not_visible`, for a target the reader does not see, the owner's count only; and `outside_lens`, for a target the reader sees that the lens neither kept nor cut: past its hops, outside its types, the lens element itself, a message its mail part took. For a target the lens cut, the lens's own reason wins over `not_visible` (an anchor the reader cannot see is the lens's `anchor_not_visible`), each reason owner-only where the lens's is. `outside_lens` is the graph's reason, not a lens's: a lens's `cut` is about elements it dropped, this is about a link from a kept node to an element the lens never reached, which only a graph produces by walking edges, and it leaks nothing, since the target is in the reader's view. It is what lets a tool draw "3 more links" at a lens's edge. A reader that is not the owner never carries `hidden`, `not_visible` or the lens's owner-only reasons in `edges_cut`, not even as zero, as in a lens.
- **Out by default, a flag brings each back.** Room elements (`rooms`), hidden ones (`hidden`), proposed ones (`proposed`), and map space, Map, Pin, Marker and Zone (`map_space`). The reading aids (rule 4's charter, guide and domains, by the pair and the mark `x_charter_of`; the pair without the mark is lore and a node like any other) are out of an unlensed graph too, counted `reading_aid`, and no flag brings them back: a lens that names the pair keeps them, as it keeps a room pair. Each flag is a boolean, false by default; a node it brings back carries its mark (map space carries none: it is out for size, not for standing). `hidden` and `proposed` are the owner's alone: from any other rung the flag is refused by name before anything is read, never quietly ignored, and no flag ever reaches a public reading, which is the leak law. When several flags are refused at once, the first unmet one in the order `hidden`, `proposed`, `rooms`, `map_space` is named, so a public reader asking everything is refused as `hidden`. **A flag never widens a lens**: with a lens, the flags add only what the lens's own rules already kept (a lens that names a room pair keeps its room elements whatever `rooms` says), so a lens means one thing everywhere, and only an unlensed graph is widened.
- **Reading as.** The owner reading `as` another reader is judged by that reader's rung: the owner as a contributor asking `hidden` is refused, and the owner's own counts stay, as on the resolved route.
- **A stable order.** Nodes by id, compared by code point. Edges by source id, then field name, both by code point, then `index`. A reader that returns the same set gives the same bytes, and a tool's picture keyed by id is stable across reads.
- **A lossless reading keeps five things**: the mirror pairs as stored; list order (`index`); unset as absence, never 0 or a default; `x_` values byte for byte; the server's fields and the World. The graph carries ids, names and the two presentation keys; a tool that wants a node's full body reads it from the snapshot by id, as `resolveLens`'s rows already give it.
- **The invariants, each with its test.** One edge per (source, field, target, index). No edge to an id that is not a node. The same snapshot, reader and options give the same output, byte for byte. A public reader's graph holds no room, hidden or proposed node and no count of them, whatever is asked. A known-of node has no edge out. A lens's graph has exactly the lens's `kept` as its nodes. Flags refused from the wrong rung are refused before any read.
- **The fixture.** `lens.fixture.json` carries a `graph` kind: each case is self-contained, with its own small `world` (the elements as the owner is served them, `hidden: true` on an element the hidden mark keeps, where the page's seven named worlds carry a world-level `hidden` list instead, a room element by its room pair and `x_room_of`, a proposed one by `x_review_proposed`), its `reader` (`rung`, its `character`, a guest's `whole` and `known_of`, the owner's `as`), its `options` (the flags, `lens` as an id or a body, and `clock` for a mail part's window), and the `want`: `nodes` in order with their marks, `edges` in order, `edges_cut`, the lens's `cut` and `unreadable`, or, for a flag asked from the wrong rung, `refused` naming the flag. A graph case carries its own world rather than naming one of the page's seven because the order case needs ids above U+FFFF that no uuid can carry. **Words.** **lens** (the saved reading) · **anchor** (an element a lens points at, through its links) · **hop** (one step along any link, both ways) · **window** (the world's own time units before the reading's date) · **template** (a lens with no anchors) · **library** (a world of templates) · **the bench** (the Lenses page on onlyworlds.com) · **the seat** (the core every reading starts from; not a lens) · **node** (an element in a graph) · **edge** (one link, one field, one target) · **mark** (why a node that is out by default is in) · **edges cut** (links left out because their target is not a node).
