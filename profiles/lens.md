# Convention profile: the lens

**What it is.** A lens is a saved way of reading a world. The 22 types say what a world holds. A profile says what a convention IS (a message, a room element, a work item: its fields and what every reader must agree on). A lens says what to READ (which things, how far, how much). Every tool already has an unsaved one (the context skill's recipe, a pack's choice, Atlas's filters, what an NPC may talk about); a lens makes it one element every tool reads. A convention on the 22 types: nothing here adds a type or a field to the standard, and a tool that has never read this page sees an ordinary Construct with one extension key it keeps byte for byte.

**What is core is not a lens; it is the seat.** Every reading starts from the same four things, whatever the world: who I am (my Character's sheet), what names me, my mail, what changed since I last read. That is the draught's base today and nobody configures it. Lenses add to the seat and never take from it. **A lens only narrows**: what a seat starts with is the lens applied inside what the seat may already read, never past it. So a lens is never a way around a guest slice, and the security story is one sentence.

**What it is not.** A profile (what a thing is); a policy in the old sense (what a seat may: that is the rung and the seat); the seat's core; a graph (a graph draws a set; a lens produces one). Nor a graph profile (nodes, edges, how a Relation is drawn), a map-space profile (what a pixel means), or a world's "now" (`as_of` here points at it).

**The pair.** Profile `lens`, the pair `construct` + supertype `Lens`, the type and the supertype each trimmed and folded like the room profile's pairs (`profiles/room.md`): `" lens "` matches, `"Lenses"` does not. A lens is a **Construct**: its definition fits ("abstract or conceptual structures that help explain or organize how the world works") and its fields are the lens's fields already: `founder` (who made it), `custodian` (the Institution that keeps it), `status` (draft, live, retired), `reach` (who it is for), `rationale` (why it exists, in words), and under Involves a link list to every type that can be an anchor. **Supertype `Lens`, subtype free** (a pack may set it: `Mail`, `Role`, `Chapter`); the subtype `Template` is reserved (*The template*, below).

**The anchors.** **The links are the anchors and nothing else**: "Product" links the Product institution, the roadmap construct and the two people who own it. The anchor fields are the Construct's Involves links in the schema's order, less `titles`: `characters`, `objects`, `locations`, `species`, `creatures`, `institutions`, `traits`, `collectives`, `zones`, `abilities`, `phenomena`, `languages`, `families`, `relations`, `constructs`, `events`, `narratives`. `founder` and `custodian` say who made and keeps the lens and are never anchors.
- **The umbrella.** A lens that links other lenses through `constructs` is the umbrella ("Office" = Mail + People + Decisions). A Construct in `constructs` that is not a lens is an anchor like any other.
- **A Title's defaults.** `titles` on a lens names the Titles it is the default for ("everyone with the Engineer title starts with Product and Decisions"). They are not anchors.

**Where a seat's choice lives.** A seat's own choice of lenses is the seat's, not the world's: it lives on the membership in keel, where the dashboard edits it and the draught reads it, like a seat's Character today; no Relation per use. A folder world has no membership, so the Character carries `x_lens_uses` (a list of lens ids, a key of this profile) for an AI reading the folder (the context skill); Atlas lists every lens in the world and keeps the view choice on the computer. When both exist, the membership wins: keel reads `x_lens_uses` only when the membership has no choice, and the dashboard saves a choice to the membership, never to the key, so the list is never kept in two places. A membership that has chosen no lenses has made a choice.

**Three levels and no more**: the world's shared lenses (made by the owner or a keeper, custodian the company), a Title's defaults (through `titles` on the lens), a seat's own choice (which shared lenses it uses, plus one private lens of its own). Public or not is the element's own visibility, nothing extra. **The pin is version zero**: a pinned row is a lens with the pinned elements as anchors, `depth` 0, the seat's own private lens.

**The template.** **A template is a lens with no anchors and subtype `Template`**: types, depth, window, show, mail, and nothing pointed at; it resolves to nothing until copied and pointed. Copied into a world and pointed at that world's things, it is a lens. That is how a lens travels between worlds and people: it is an element, it exports with a world, and a template library is a world whose elements are templates. A reader resolves a lens whose subtype is `Template` to nothing, anchors or not; pointing a copy means giving it anchors and another subtype.

**The field.** The recipe that is not links lives in one extension field, `x_lens`, a JSON object on the lens. Every key is optional; `null` reads as absent; an empty `x_lens` (or none) is a lens of depth 1 over its anchors with no window and the draught's default budget. A key this page does not name is ignored (a later serial may add one). A value outside the table makes the lens unreadable: the reader names the key and resolves nothing, never a guess. The prefix `x_lens_` is reserved. The keys are the draught's own words where it has one, with the draught's meaning:

| Key | What | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `depth` | how far from the anchors, in hops | 0 (the anchors only), 1 (and what links to or from them; the default), 2 (the profile allows it; a reader serves 0 and 1 and counts what the second hop reaches as `hops_not_served`) |
| `types` | which types may be kept | a list of the API's lowercase type slugs (`character`); a room pair as `narrative/message` beside `narrative` (the room profile's nine pairs, type and supertype lowercase, a slash between); each word trimmed and folded; empty or absent = any type, and no room element |
| `window` | how far back | a whole number from 0 of the world's own time units before the reading's `as_of` (a world that counts years counts its window in years): an element is inside when its date is at or after `as_of` minus the window. Absent = always. Never beside `from` or `to` |
| `from` | an absolute range's start, in the world's dates | a whole number, inclusive; alone, the range runs to `as_of` |
| `to` | the range's end | a whole number after `from`, exclusive (the range is closed-open); never alone; a `to` after `as_of` is clipped to `as_of`: the fence stays the upper edge |
| `undated` | what the fence does with an element of a dated type that carries no date | `include` (the default: kept) or `exclude` (cut and counted `undated`) |
| `show` | how much of each kept element | `full` (the body as the reader is served it; the default), `first_line` (`id`, `type`, `name`, and `description` up to its first line break), `name` (`id`, `type`, `name`) |
| `budget` | the limit | a whole number from 1 to 1000, counted in elements; default 50, the draught's |
| `mail` | the mail rule | `unread_full_read_titles` (unread in full, read as subject, sender, date; a rolling window; one totals line), `all` (every message in full, the same window and totals), or `none` (the default) |
| `notes` | a sentence per anchor | an object from an anchor's id to one sentence, the lens's reason for that anchor; a kept anchor's row carries it as `note`; a note for an id that is not an anchor is ignored and counted. Not a second description: the lens's `description` says what the lens is for, a note says why this anchor is in it |

**Resolution: the rule for readers.** The reader is one pure function over a world snapshot, `resolveLens(world, lens, reader)`, which returns the kept elements in order, the cut counted by reason, and, when the lens names a mail rule, the mail part. It never writes.
1. **Visibility before resolution.** The resolver takes the reader's own view of the world; a hidden or proposed element is not there to hop through. Keel's draught already works this way: every gather is scoped to the reader, and an item counts only if the body that reader is served carries the link. A known-of stub is in the view: it is kept as served and nothing hops out of it (a stub carries no links). A proposed element (`x_review_proposed` an object, the review profile) is in no reader's view.
2. **A hop reaches both ways**, to and from an anchor: one hop from an element reaches every element its served body links to and every element whose served body links to it.
3. **A cycle, and the umbrella**: only the umbrella's own `constructs` links are entered, each child once (a visited set), the union in link order, each child resolved by its own recipe; a Lens reached by a hop is kept as an element and never entered; a lens that only reaches itself resolves to its anchors. The umbrella's own anchors come first, then each child's kept list, each element once; the umbrella's `budget` caps the union. The lens being read, and every lens entered, is never in its own result.
4. **A hop never crosses a room element** into lore or out of it; a lens that wants the room names a room pair in `types` (`narrative/message`); an anchor that is itself a room element is kept, and nothing hops out of it. A room element a hop reaches is kept only when `types` names its pair, and is cut as `types` otherwise.
5. **The budget's order**: anchors first in the lens's own link order (the schema's field order, then each field's array order), then each hop's elements by type in the schema's order, then within a type newest first (`updated_at`), then by id (by id alone, a limit can keep the old version of a fact and cut the new); the cut is reported as a count by reason, never silent. The schema's type order is `schema.json`'s: ability, collective, character, construct, creature, event, family, institution, language, law, location, map, marker, narrative, object, phenomenon, pin, relation, species, title, trait, zone. The budget keeps the first elements in this order and cuts the rest.
6. **An anchor the reader cannot see** is skipped, never an error. The count ("1 anchor not visible") is the owner's view only; every other reader gets the lens as if the anchor were not there, since the count would tell a guest that something hidden exists. A lens that names an anchor is never read as a filter, whoever reads it: one whose only anchors are hidden from the reader resolves to nothing. When keel serves a lens to a reader that cannot see an anchor, it leaves that id out and names the field in `x_served_trimmed`, as it does for a message's recipients (keel marks messages today; a lens's mark ships with the draught's reading of lenses); a reader holding only served bodies reads a lens whose `x_served_trimmed` names an anchor field as anchored.
7. **A lens with no anchors and only types** is a filter over the whole visible world, unless it is a template: a template carries subtype `Template` and resolves to nothing; a live lens with no anchors and no `Template` subtype is a filter. A filter keeps, in the order of rule 5, every element of its `types` the reader can see; elements of other types are not selected and not counted.
8. **The upper edge is keel's `as_of` fence**: the doors sit on `as_of`, the reading's date (absent: the world's now), not on `window`, which only reaches back. A known-of stub serves no date, so the resolver fences on the element's own date, never on the stub's null (an Event after `as_of` reached as a stub is cut, `after_as_of`). `undated` is a key of `x_lens`: under the fence an undated element is kept (`include`, the draught's default, as the fence's doors serve undated Events and undated knowledge Narratives) or cut and counted ("undated: 3") when the lens says `exclude`, so the leak becomes a counted cut. The **date table**, type to its date field: `birth_date` (Character, Creature), `founding_date` (Institution, Location), `formation_date` (Collective), `date` (Law), `grant_date` (Title), `start_date` (Construct, Event, Narrative, Relation, Zone); the ten types with no date field (Ability, Family, Language, Map, Marker, Object, Phenomenon, Pin, Species, Trait) are timeless and never fenced. The fence, `undated`, `window`, `from` and `to` all read this table. The fence and `undated` apply to anchors too; `types` and `window` apply to what a hop or a filter reaches, never to an anchor the lens names. A date compares at its stated precision and is never filled to a day; every date field in the standard is a whole number in the world's `time_basic_unit`, so its precision is the world's unit. A Character's own sheet is never fenced, and that is not a lens case: the seat is core and no lens takes from it.
9. **The mail rule reads the message profile** (`profiles/message.md`): unread by the reader's own Read mark, the sender verified; the totals line counts only what this reader may see. The mail part holds the messages addressed to the reader's Character and not sent by it, in `created_at` order; the window is thirty days on `created_at` (mail is dated by the clock, not the world's calendar) before the reading's time, capped by the lens's `budget` (the newest kept, the oldest cut first and counted, each by reason); unread messages in full, read ones as a title (`id`, `name`, `narrator`, `created_at`), or every one in full under `all`. The totals count the unread and the read the reader may see, before the window and the cap, so the cut says how many older messages the world's messages route still holds. A message in the mail part is not repeated among the kept. A reader with no Character has no mail.
10. **Totals and counts are the reader's**, never the world's: "212 more" is 212 the reader could open.

**A Relation is traversed as an edge**: a hop that lands on a Relation also reaches every element the Relation's own link fields name, in the same hop (never elements that link to the Relation, never on through a second Relation in that hop); a Relation that is itself an anchor reaches its parties at hop 1, as any element does. The Relation carries the hop whether or not `types` keeps it; a party the reader cannot see is not reached.

**The cut reasons, one closed list.** Each cut element is counted once, under the first reason that applies (the fence, then `types`, then the window, then the budget):
- `budget`: past the limit, in the order of rule 5.
- `types`: reached, of a type (or a room pair) the lens does not keep.
- `window`: dated before the window, or outside `from` and `to`.
- `undated`: of a dated type with no date, under `exclude`.
- `after_as_of`: dated after the reading's `as_of`, by the element's own date.
- `anchor_not_visible`: an anchor the reader cannot see. The owner's count only.
- `not_visible`: a non-anchor a hop would reach that the reader's view leaves out (hidden by its keeper, outside a guest's slice, or proposed). The owner's count only: the owner reading as another reader, or as itself, where it counts what is proposed.
- `hops_not_served`: what a hop past depth 1 would reach.

A reader that is not the owner never carries `anchor_not_visible` or `not_visible`, not even as zero.

**The invariants, each with its test.**
- **A lens's links are filtered per reader like any other link**; a public lens never shows a private anchor's name. Test: a planted lens built to leak, drawn as owner, contributor, guest and a seat; each result a subset of the owner's; the planted name red before the walk is believed (the fixture's planted world; keel's hidden-mark walk, the lens version).
- **A public picture of a lens is drawn from the public read only**: the cut-and-why list is owner-only; the public view shows only what the public reader's own resolution keeps; text leaks what a link cannot, so counts and reasons are the reader's. Test: bake the public picture and page twice, with the private anchor in the world and without; the two are byte-identical; a guest's or keyless answer never holds "not visible" or a reason count for one (the fixture holds the reading's half; the bake is the picture's own test).
- **The hop stops at the reader's edge, not at the count.** Test: public A, hidden P, public B; anchor A, two hops; the guest never gets B (its only path crosses P), the owner does; P's absence never moves the guest's counts (the planted world: the owner counts B as `hops_not_served` until a second hop is served).
- **A lens drawn does not move the world**: the layout is baked per world, a lens changes weight and visibility. Test: the baked layout's bytes before and after any lens are equal (the fixture holds the reading's half: the world is identical before and after every reading).
- **One truth**: a seat's lenses are read from keel by every tool; the dashboard has no second editor. Test: the dashboard's sign-in walk asserts the page writes to no route but the named ones, and the lenses it shows equal keel's resolved route for the same seat (the fixture holds where the choice lives: the membership wins).

**The planted lens for the leak walk**: a throwaway world with Ada and Brin (public), Hollow Keep (public Location), Secret Heir (hidden Character), Vault Ledger (hidden Object), a public Relation "Ada's ward" linking Ada and the Heir; links Ada to Hollow Keep, Ada to Heir, Heir to Brin, Hollow Keep to Vault Ledger. The lens: public, anchors Ada and Vault Ledger (a hidden anchor in a public lens), `depth` 2, any types, `show` full, `budget` 6. Readers: owner, contributor, guest, an agent seat. Red: the Heir or the Ledger named anywhere in a non-owner result (counts, cut reasons, alt text, the page and picture bake); Brin in the guest's answer; the Relation carrying the Heir in as its other party for a reader who cannot see him; a count equal to the owner's cut; any result not a subset of the owner's. Controls: a resolver that hides after resolving, one that counts the whole world, one that returns empty, and the lens with the hidden anchor removed (green). A name written into a public lens's rationale is amber, listed per owner.

**Defaults: what a pack ships.** A note, not a rule. Which lenses come on by default is the pack's job: each set-up pack ships its starter lenses as elements, as the company pack ships its Guide Construct today. The first sets, drafted: **company**: Mail, Decisions (Laws and Events, 30 days, names), People (the roster and tenures), Work; **story**: What my character knows (her knowledge entries, the events she was at, her relations, the places she has been), What the narrator knows (everything, hidden kept hidden), This chapter (a window around a date); **ai-home**: Mail, Projects. A user never meets a world type; they meet a world that came with three lenses on and a page to change them.

**What a writer must do.** A lens names its founder; a template carries no anchors; `status` retired means no seat starts with it. Write `x_lens` whole and keep it byte for byte, like every extension field; never write `x_served_trimmed` (keel sets it, and refuses a body that carries it). A copy into another world carries its `x_lens` and no anchors unless the target holds them.

**Words.** **lens** (the saved reading) · **anchor** (an element a lens points at, through its links) · **hop** (one step along any link, both ways) · **window** (the world's own time units before the reading's date) · **template** (a lens with no anchors) · **library** (a world of templates) · **the bench** (the Lenses page on onlyworlds.com) · **the seat** (the core every reading starts from; not a lens). Public copy says lenses and the Lenses page; the toolkit's old "lenses" are reports; Little Lens is a tool's name and stays its own.

**Where the definition is read from.** The packages' reader is `resolveLens(world, lens, reader)` (the Python package `resolve_lens`), beside `isLens(element)` and `isTemplate(element)`, and follows in their next releases, as the message and room profiles' readers went; keel reads the same profile, and the bench shows keel's reading beside the package's for the same lens: if they differ, one has a bug. The conformance fixture beside this page, `lens.fixture.json`, holds the cases every reader must agree on: the pair and the template, the legal values of `x_lens`, four small worlds (a year world, an office of lenses, the planted world, a post room) with their readers, a resolution case per rule and per key, the invariants' cases, and where a seat's choice lives. keel's reference reader is a test that replays it. A reader conforms when it passes every case.

**Ruled**: 2026-10-26, the design read and ruled; written down 2026-10-27 as this page and its fixture, with the readings the design left open taken as their leans (`from` and `to` beside `window`; `notes` per anchor; the budget counted in elements).
