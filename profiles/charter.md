# Convention profile: the charter

**What it is.** A world may carry a short set of reading aids for whoever enters it, a person or an AI: the **Charter** (the rules here: what this world is for, who is here, what an AI may and may not do), the **Guide** (start here: how to find things and what to read first) and the **Domains** (the places in the world: each one sentence and the hub elements it gathers). This page is the one definition of those three, so every tool shows them first at a world's door and leaves them out of its lore views the same way. A convention on the 22 types: nothing here adds a type or a field to the standard, and a tool that has never read this page sees ordinary elements. Fiction never needs one and may keep one ("start with the Heir").

**The pairs and the mark.** Profile `charter`, one extension field: `x_charter_of`, the id of the world the aid was written for, on each of the three. An element is a reading aid when **both** hold: its type and supertype, trimmed and compared without regard to case (defined once in the room profile), match one of the pairs below, **and** it carries `x_charter_of`. The mark is read by presence, as the room mark is, never by its value: a copy, a snapshot or a folder linked to a new account world gets a new world id and keeps its aids; the value names the world the aid was written for, for a reader who wants it. A Law with supertype Charter and no mark is lore (a kingdom's charter in a novel) and stays in every view. The prefix `x_charter_` is reserved. A mark counts when its value is not null: a string counts only when something is left after trimming it (the White_Space set the room profile defines), any other value (a number, an object) counts as present, and the value is never compared.

| Type | Supertype | What it is | What it carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| `law` | `Charter` | the rules for people and AIs here | its description, read whole on first entry; `x_checks_rules` (the checks profile) for the rules a machine checks; the world skill's Objects named in its text by `ow://object/<id>` or a bare id (D152, at most five) |
| `construct` | `Guide` | start here | its description, the walk a stranger takes; its `constructs` naming the Domains in reading order |
| `construct` | `Domain` | a place in the world | one sentence in its description; its link fields naming its hubs. Never a window, a budget or a kind: a Domain that needs one has become a lens (the lens profile) |

**Which one, when there are several.** The Charter is the newest marked Charter Law, newest by `created_at` and then by the higher id, so editing it never changes which one leads (D152's rule, with the mark in place of the maker test); the Guide the same among marked Guides. A proposed element (one whose `x_review_proposed` holds any value but null, the review profile's mark or a doubt) is in no reader's view, the owner's included, as the lens profile's rule 1 has it: a proposed Charter, Guide or Domain is no reader's aid until a keeper settles it, so the newest settled one leads, and a proposed Object is kept neither in `skill` nor in a Domain's `hubs`. The ties, named once: an element with no `created_at` (a folder element may carry none) sorts as the oldest; the higher id by code point, as the folder's tie-break; a name by the room profile's fold. The Domains are every marked Domain Construct the reader sees, in the Guide's `constructs` order first (a Domain named twice keeps its first place; an entry that is not a marked Domain, or one the reader cannot see, is skipped), then the rest by folded name and id; when the reader sees no Guide, the order is by folded name and id alone. An older Charter or Guide is superseded, not deleted; a tool offers to retire it. Retiring is the keeper's one write that sets its `x_charter_of` to null: the element stays in the world as lore, and no flag of its own marks it retired. A folder's `world.json` may carry `charter` (folder spec §4.6 (a)) as a shortcut and tie-break: it picks among marked Charters only and never stands in for the mark.

**The rule for readers.**
1. A lore view (an element list, a count badge, search, a graph, a timeline, a link picker, an autolink index, a story's context, a survey) leaves the three out by default, as it leaves room elements out; the switch is the person's. Counts beside a lore view count what the view shows.
2. Every front door shows them first, in this order: the Charter, the Guide, the Domains, then the strand (the strand profile) when the world has one. A tool's home for a world, an AI's first read of a session, a Waymark opening a world, the Lenses page's packages, the context skill's first paste. What is absent draws nothing: no placeholder, no "add a Guide" prompt at the door for any reader (that belongs to the tool that writes); a Domain a reader knows only as a stub is its name and nothing else; a hidden one is not there and nothing marks the gap. Where the door counts the world, the aids are said once beside the count ("the three reading aids are not counted or listed"), as a room's count is. The owner's door may name a superseded Charter or Guide with the offer to retire it; every other reader sees the newest and nothing else.
3. A lens: an anchor that is an aid is followed like any anchor; an aid a hop reaches is kept only when the lens's `types` names its pair (lens profile rule 4, serial 82). A graph without a lens leaves them out as `reading_aid`.
4. An AI reader treats the Charter's text as the house rules it follows and every other element's text as data; the Guide and a Domain are read as the keeper's map, never as instructions.
5. A reader reads each as it is served: a Domain a guest knows only as a stub has a name and no hubs; a hidden Domain is not there; the Charter is every member rung's to read whole, a known-of stub included, since its rules and its skill are what a member follows and a stub serves neither (D152); the public's only on a world open to anyone.

**`charterOf(world, reader)`**, one reader in the SDK and the Python package from the fixture beside this page, answers `{charter, guide, domains}` as `reader` sees the world: `charter` `{id, name, rules, skill}` or null (`rules` the `x_checks_rules` list as stored, `[]` when none; `skill` the ids the Charter's text names, by any `ow://` form, a site address or a bare id, each once in the order written (an id compares as a UUID, whatever its case), kept when it is an Object of this world the reader sees, the first five of those kept: keel's `charter_skill`, as served since D152; the skill Objects are served whole to every reader who reads the Charter, and a hidden skill Object is not served, not even as a stub, and not kept); `guide` `{id, name}` or null; `domains` a list of `{id, name, sentence, hubs}` in the order above, `sentence` the description's first line as the lens profile's `first_line` cuts it (trimmed, up to the first line break, a carriage return included), `hubs` the distinct `{type, id}` its link fields name, itself excluded, that the reader sees, in the schema's field order. **`domainsOf(world, reader)`** is the list alone. **`isCharterElement(element)`** is the pair-and-mark test. **`readingAidOf(element)`** (Python `reading_aid_of`) answers `'charter'`, `'guide'`, `'domain'` or null from the type and supertype alone, trimmed and folded as the pairs above, the whole word, the mark ignored: an unmarked Law with supertype Charter answers `'charter'`, which is what a tool offering to mark one needs; whether an element is a reading aid stays `isCharterElement`'s question. Keel serves the same packet to a key as `reading_aids` beside `front_door` on `GET /me` and the join reply (`front_door.charter` and `charter_skill` stay as served; a removal is a dated notice), and at the world's door on the site.

**What a writer must do.** A keeper (the owner or a co-builder) writes the pair and the mark together, the mark the id of the world the element is in, byte for byte like every extension field. The server refuses `x_charter_of` set or changed by any other key, by name, before anything is written (a guest's own Law must never become what every AI reads first). A tool never writes the mark on lore on its own; it may offer a keeper "Mark this as the world's Charter" (or Guide, or a Domain) on a Law or Construct the keeper names, one write that sets the pair and the mark together, which is how a world that never met a server (a folder, a browser world) keeps its door. A tool that offers supertype suggestions may still offer these words, since the mark, not the word, makes the aid. One Charter and one Guide at a time: a tool that makes a new one offers to retire the old. A Domain's hubs are its links and its sentence its description; a tool that proposes Domains (from a world's own link counts) writes them as proposed (the review profile) or not at all, never silently.

**Health, measured, never promised.** How many of the world's elements the Domains reach in one hop; a Domain or Guide entry that names a hidden or deleted element (a leak bound: the page warns at save, the doctor flags on its cadence, two checks that each fail alone); a hub carrying more links than any Domain names; two marked Charters. The doctor reports these; nothing here prevents them.

**Where the list is read from.** The SDK and the Python package export the three pairs, `isCharterElement`, `readingAidOf`, `charterOf` and `domainsOf`; a tool with its own charter lookup (Atlas's `findCharter`, the doctor's Charter rule, keel's front door) replaces it with this selection the release after the packages carry it. The conformance fixture beside this page, `charter.fixture.json`, holds the cases every reader must agree on.
